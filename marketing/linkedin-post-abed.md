# Abed's LinkedIn Launch Post

I need your help. Everyone should see this.

Lebanon is displaced again.

In 2024, I watched hundreds of thousands of families get forced from their homes overnight. NGOs didn't know who needed what. Field agents were collecting data on paper. Housing offers weren't reaching the people who needed them. Emergency numbers were scattered across WhatsApp groups.

The coordination layer was missing.

So we built Nasna.

Then the ceasefire came. People went home. We thought we were done.

We weren't.

In 2026, the displacement crisis is back. And this time we're ready.

Nasna (ناسنا) means "Our People."

It's a humanitarian aid platform that connects displaced families in Lebanon with the NGOs and resources they need, in real time.

Here's what it does:

Field agents register families through a form that works fully offline. No internet? It queues and syncs automatically when reconnected.

NGOs get a live filtered feed of only the cases that match their coverage area and aid types. No noise, no irrelevant requests.

A live map shows every active displacement site, aid center, and NGO coverage zone across Lebanon, with real-time capacity data.

A dedicated hotlines directory lists every emergency number in Lebanon — police, Red Cross, mental health, humanitarian orgs — one tap to call.

Anyone can offer housing, browse available housing, find emergency contacts, or register a family. No login required.

Phone numbers of displaced families are never exposed to NGOs. Privacy is enforced at the schema level, not as an afterthought.

Built with React 19, TypeScript, Firebase, Leaflet, and Tailwind. Fully offline-first. Available in Arabic, English, and French.

Here's how you can help, pick whatever fits:

You run or know an NGO in Lebanon: register at nasna.world. It's free, always will be.

You know displaced families or field agents on the ground: send them nasna.world directly.

You want to support financially: nasna.world/donate

You can't do any of the above right now: just share this post. That's enough. Every share puts Nasna in front of the NGO or family that needs it.

Nasna is fully open source.

The entire codebase is on GitHub: https://github.com/voxire/nasna

If you want to contribute:

1. Fork the repo and clone it locally
2. Check the open issues for good first contributions, or open one if you have an idea
3. Make your changes and open a pull request against main
4. We review and merge directly, no bureaucracy, no waiting list

We're looking for developers, designers, translators, and anyone with domain knowledge in humanitarian work. If what you build helps one family find shelter faster, it belongs in this project.
